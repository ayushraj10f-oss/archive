Ajit Doval on the problems created by democracy

Excerpt from Ajit Doval’s remarks on governance, delivered on Rashtriya Ekta Diwas.

Transcription (excerpt):

“What has happened is that while democracy has proved to be one of the most effective and enduring systems of governance, it has created its own problems.”
“It leads to partisan politics. There are divisions. Division is a very strange thing. If there are 100 people and I have a following of 25, I can come to power if I can divide the rest into groups that are each smaller than 20.”
“It is not an aspiration to make it 51, but an aspiration to divide the rest of the society into as many fragments as possible. That fragmentation is the danger.”
“In democracies, money plays a role — whether legitimate or illegitimate. Your idealism, vision, thought, and patriotism must have strong support from money.”
“There is often an eclipsing of national interest by vested interest — not necessarily corruption, but smaller, localised priorities overriding the larger national interest.”
“We must examine our systems — laws, rules, procedures — and make them more people-friendly. Policies must meet aspirations. Basic needs must be addressed. People must feel secure.”
